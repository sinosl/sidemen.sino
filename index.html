The idea

Position GoPro as the Sidemen's point-of-view camera, so the product generates the content rather than interrupting it: GoPro-filmed Sidemen Sunday challenges, "Player Cam" at the next Charity Match, and short-form cut-downs.

The proof

The affinity already exists. Players such as WillNE and Miniminter have brought their own GoPros to the Charity Match as personal "player cams". The official 2026 match post featuring one was not a GoPro advert: the brand earned the placement organically. This deal makes it commercial.

The deal

A £350k fixed fee (c.42% of media value), product in kind and 10% commission on code sales. The discount lowers GoPro's upfront risk; performance pay preserves the Sidemen's upside.

Upside

The same framework prioritises Howbout across the portfolio, and routes airtime to shorts and event moments, where its opportunity cost is lowest.

Why GoPro

The only brand whose product generates the content rather than sitting beside it. It is priced within reach of 18-25s, under-used by UK creators, and commercially motivated to act now. It has also already appeared organically: players such as WillNE and Miniminter have brought their own GoPros to the Charity Match as unsponsored "player cams". PlayStation runs it closest, but already works with many gaming creators.

How it's valued

Media value prices the attention GoPro would otherwise buy: views × the market rate per 1,000 views ($24 base, range $15-30). Event placements are discounted for GoPro's share of a busy broadcast, then uplifted for exclusivity, live reach and press.

Plus camera sales

2,220 cameras through the Sidemen code (£666k) if 0.2% of viewers visit and 3% buy. Only incremental sales count: GoPro keeps £157k of gross profit after excluding buyers who would have purchased anyway.

Built in, not bolted on

The challenge videos carry 55% of the value because the camera is the format. Remove the product and the video no longer exists: the strongest defence against ad fatigue.

Why this shape

GoPro cut marketing by 37%, so a market-rate fee would likely stall in procurement. Pricing the fixed fee at c.42% of media value lowers the hurdle, while commission (£67k) shifts part of the payment to outcomes GoPro can verify.

For the Sidemen

About £417k in cash plus kit, with uncapped upside if sales outperform. Accepting performance pay also signals confidence in product-audience fit, which strengthens the negotiating position.

Protection

Instalments tied to delivery milestones, backed by a minimum-views guarantee and a make-good video, so neither side carries the full risk of underperformance.

Order value

Biggest drivers

The result is most sensitive to the market rate per 1,000 views and main-channel views. Both are pricing inputs, and both are verifiable from the Sidemen's analytics and agency rate cards.

Sales hardly matter

Higher sales lift GoPro's gross profit but also its commission, so the two largely offset. The structure is deliberately self-hedging.

Worst case

With every input at its low value at once, GoPro receives 0.81x; at high values, 3.70x. The downside needs everything to go wrong together, so a views guarantee is proportionate protection.

Risks, covered

Constrained budget: mitigated by a below-market fee, kit in lieu of cash and performance pay.
Advert fatigue: the Sidemen retain creative control; GoPro appears only where it is genuinely used.
Under-delivery: a minimum-views guarantee with a make-good video.
Player Cam logistics: early safety and broadcast sign-off with the production company.
Model risk: benchmark inputs replaced with first-party data before pricing is agreed.
